I bought this book almost by accident. I was shopping on an online bookstore and needed to reach a certain amount to get a discount. After quickly checking the table of contents, I decided to give it a try. Unexpectedly, the content turned out to be very practical.

### A Practical Book vs. A Conceptual Book

The author repeatedly emphasizes that this is a practical book, not a conceptual one.

1. **The techniques themselves are concrete actions, not descriptions filled with empty words.** This is also why people often say that even after reading many business books, they still cannot really apply them at work.
2. We should not wait until finishing the whole book before thinking about whether to practice it. If we only start thinking about practice after reading, the result is often staying in the same place. The motivation to act after finishing a book usually does not last long. Therefore, I think the better attitude is: **try it first and think later**.
3. Many books on the market are more like “over-interpretation books.” They focus on readability and entertainment, but often use vague words that are difficult to turn into action. For example: “make up your mind,” “have a strong awareness,” or “pour in your passion.” These phrases sound correct, but they are not easy to execute.

### The One-Page Thinking Method

Most of the techniques in this book revolve around the author’s “one-page thinking method.” The core idea is to use one sheet of paper to quickly grasp the key points of a problem. The author also quotes Konosuke Matsushita’s writings to support his ideas, which makes the book more interesting to read.

In the following sections, I will focus mainly on the one-page method itself, and then use Matsushita’s ideas as supporting material. I think this structure is easier to understand than following the book’s original order.

## Positive Focus

### How to Use the One-Page Method

I want to explain the one-page method directly with an example. First, prepare an A4 paper and three different colored pens. Of course, if you prefer, Word or Excel should also work.

| |  | | |
|-|-|-|-|
|<font color="#3f8">How is my physical condition?</font>|Often have headaches|Runny nose|Have cavities|
|Stiff shoulders|<font color="#f00">Slept well</font>|Sat all day||
|Itchy eyes|Sore throat|<font color="#f00">Good digestion</font>||
|<font color="#f00">Hair has grown longer</font>|Feel like I may catch a cold|Gained some weight||

The steps are simple:

1. Divide the paper into a 4x4 table, which gives 16 boxes in total. Write the question in the top-left box.
2. Fill the remaining boxes with your answers. Try to write as much as possible, but you do not need to fill every box.
3. Use red to **mark the positive items**. If we only pick negative items, the brain will keep focusing on negative things, and this can easily become a bad loop.

### Taking It One Step Further

We should also look for things that can be rephrased and included from a more positive angle.

| |  | | |
|-|-|-|-|
|<font color="#3f8">What happened yesterday?</font>|<font color="#f00">New project meeting</font>|Proposal planning and presentation|Replying to many emails|
|Worked overtime until 9 p.m.|Got blamed because preparation was not enough <font color="#f00">(I will not make the same mistake next time)</font>|Attended regular meetings|<font color="#f00">Confirmed future tasks</font>|
|<font color="#f00">Proposed new ideas</font>|Had lunch with a vendor|<font color="#f00">Wrote a thank-you email</font>||
|Rearranged delayed work <font color="#f00">(Now I do not need to worry about it later)</font>|Helped a colleague|Handled customer complaints <font color="#f00">(The customer was satisfied in the end)</font>||

With more practice, we may gradually become better at seeing things from a more positive angle.

### The Goal of the One-Page Method

The first time we practice this method, it may take longer. But the target is to finish the whole process within three minutes. If the process takes too long, it loses part of its meaning.

I also think it is worth trying this for a month. If we use the one-page method every day to focus on positive things, we should be able to notice some growth.

### My Thoughts

1. I think dividing the paper into a 4x4 table helps us focus. Compared with writing 16 items in one long list, the table format makes it easier to grasp the key points quickly.
2. “Positive focus” is a very important idea. I think it is similar to the [Pareto principle](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E5%B8%95%E7%B4%AF%E6%89%98%E6%B3%95%E5%89%87), which means finding the most influential things and doing them first. This does not mean the less important things are useless. It means we should invest our limited resources in the right places.

## Changing the Way We Work

In this part of the book, the author tries to use Konosuke Matsushita’s ideas as the main structure, and then applies the one-page method to practice those ideas. Personally, I think this makes the book harder to read. So I prefer to list the applications of the one-page method one by one, and then use Matsushita’s writings as supplements.

### How Do We Spend Our Weekends? One Day for Rest, One Day for Growth

In *Employee心得帖*, Matsushita mentions the idea of **one day for rest and one day for growth**. The basic idea is that during a two-day weekend, we should use one day to rest and the other day to enrich ourselves.

We can use the one-page method mentioned in the previously to quickly analyze and organize our weekend activities. The steps are:

1. Write down the question.
2. Write down the answers.
3. Mark items related to **self-growth** in red.

| |  | | |
|-|-|-|-|
|<font color="#3f8">How did I spend my weekend?</font>|<font color="#f00">Worked at a café</font>|Shopping|Cleaning the house|
|Played video games|Visited a bookstore|Went to karaoke|Cooked|
|Did nothing at home|Met friends|<font color="#f00">Joined a work-related seminar</font>||
|<font color="#f00">Reading</font>|Ate good food|Did laundry||

Besides this, Matsushita also mentioned that we should contribute to people other than ourselves. In one example, many people asked why someone would buy home appliances early, even though the price usually drops after a while. Matsushita believed that these early buyers were contributing to the development of television culture by purchasing the products at a higher price.

Therefore, we can ask another question: **Which things are investments in myself, and which things are contributions to people other than myself?**

## More Applications of the One-Page Method

Below are three applications of the one-page method that I personally found useful. After reading them, I hope we can better understand the advantages behind this method and try to apply it ourselves.

### One-Page Conversation Method

The steps of the one-page conversation method are:

1. While listening to the other person, write keywords into the boxes.
2. After the conversation ends, use a pen to connect related items with arrows. For example:
   * Key people -> Section Chief XX and Mr. XX
   * Things to do today -> Next interview, several emails, internal coordination

| |  | | |
|-|-|-|-|
|<font color="#3f8">Conversation summary</font>|Section Chief XX|Final goal|Internal coordination|
|The vendor is troublesome|Mr. XX|Things to do today|Report to the boss|
|Who are the key people?|Solution methods|Several emails||
|Department Manager XX|Next interview|Prepare materials||

I think this method is quite intuitive. Usually, when listening to a report or a speech, it is not easy to immediately understand the speaker’s structure. So writing down keywords first and organizing them later should be a very common and useful approach.

### One-Page Priority Decision Method

The purpose of the one-page priority decision method is to help us quickly decide which things should be done first. The steps are:

1. Mark the items that will be **most effective after being done**. A circle can be used.
2. Mark the items that look **easy to do and easy to execute**. A triangle can be used.
3. Mark the items that **I especially want to do**. A square can be used.

Finally, decide the priority based on the number of marks each item receives.

### One-Page Reflection Method

The steps of the one-page reflection method are:

1. Write down the purpose of the work.
2. Write down what was actually done, and mark the useful items with color.
3. Write down what to do next week.

### A Small Note

One thing to notice is that this idea is different from the previous approach. Here, we do not need to fully execute every marked item with 100% effort. Doing around 60% is probably enough.

Personally, I think the one-page method combines several common theories and experiences. Its main feature is that we can do two things at the same time: **(1) brainstorming and (2) simple data processing and presentation**. If you are thinking about a problem, I would suggest trying this method.

## How to Deal With Difficult People

The third part of the book mainly discusses group applications. The previous chapters were mostly about personal use.

### Look at Strengths 70% of the Time and Weaknesses 30% of the Time

In *Business心得帖*, Matsushita mentioned that he looked at employees’ strengths rather than only their weaknesses. Of course, if there is a weakness, it should still be corrected immediately. But he suggests using 70% of our effort to look at others’ strengths, and the remaining 30% to correct their weaknesses.

We can use the one-page method to help us do this.

First, list the people we find difficult to deal with. Then choose the third most difficult person. After that, use one page to ask ourselves two questions:

1. Why is this person difficult to deal with?
2. What are this person’s strengths?

| |  | | |
|-|-|-|-|
|<font color="#3f8">Why is this person difficult to deal with?</font>|Speaks harshly|<font color="#3f8">What are this person’s strengths?</font>|Simple in some ways|
|Does not read the room|Seems to look down on me|Can make calm judgments||
|Hard to understand what this person is thinking|Looks down on people in the company|Has confidence||
|Cold and arrogant||Does not say one thing and do another||

Through this process, we should be able to find the person’s strengths. What we need to do is try to pay more attention to the strengths, instead of only focusing on the weaknesses.

## Applying the One-Page Method to Meetings

In many meetings, when a manager asks, “Does anyone have any ideas?”, the room usually becomes awkward. Sometimes even people who originally wanted to speak may stay quiet because of the atmosphere.

The author believes that to create an environment where people can speak more easily, we should follow a few rules:

1. Do not suddenly ask someone to speak.
2. Do not ask someone to speak alone.
3. Do not let only a small number of loud people speak.

### Using One Page in Meeting Discussions

The steps are:

1. The manager first explains the purpose of the meeting. For example: “In order to reduce overtime in our department, we need to discuss possible solutions.”
2. Ask every participant to write down ideas on one page. At this stage, they do not need to analyze the ideas carefully.
3. Ask participants to sort their priorities based on the following questions:
   1. Question 1: Ignoring whether it is feasible for now, which idea would be the most effective after being done? [Effectiveness]
   2. Question 2: Which idea looks easy to do and easy to execute? [Feasibility]
   3. Question 3: Which idea can gain support not only from myself, but also from colleagues in the workplace? [Applicability]
   4. Other questions: Which idea fits the workplace atmosphere better? Which idea costs less money?
4. Divide 10 people into 2 groups. Choose one person in each group to take notes. Let each group spend 20 minutes discussing their “top three proposals for reducing overtime.”
5. Write down the 6 proposals from the 2 groups, and then let everyone discuss and decide the final result.
   * In this situation, if both groups propose the same solution, both sides may feel, “I see, so they also thought about this.” This can also help create a sense of unity.

After reading this part, I feel this method should be quite effective in real meetings, although I have not had a chance to try it yet. If anyone has suggestions, I would also be happy to discuss them.
